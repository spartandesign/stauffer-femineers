Phase 2 · October deliverables

Arrive ready to build.

Use October to turn the approved proposal into a clear, buildable plan. Submit the complete package in Canvas, explain it during the lunch checkpoint, and resolve questions before the November 9 workday.

Phase 2 format: Complete the work through Canvas and a Wednesday lunch checkpoint in Room 14. Submit by Monday, October 19. Mentors review on October 20. Demonstrate your work at lunch on Wednesday, October 21.

Phase 2 rule

Plan now so November can begin with action.

No finished physical prototype is required in October. Use your iPad or a classroom Windows laptop to complete the design evidence, digital files, materials request, and first test plan.

Pathway proof

Make the technology understandable before materials are released.

Wearable Technology · Individual

Circuit and light plan

  • Front and back shirt design plus hat design
  • Battery, micro:bit, LEDs, connections, and removable electronics labeled
  • Planned color or animation sequence
  • Comfort, movement, visibility, and safe power considerations
  • First circuit or material question to test

Creative Robotics · Team of two

Interaction and mechanism plan

  • Future user and need statement
  • Input → decision → at least two outputs → reset system map
  • Three to five frame visitor storyboard
  • Hummingbird parts, structure, movement, and service access labeled
  • First interaction or mechanism question to test

November 9 launch plan

Prepare a small and honest first test.

  • Write one question the first prototype must answer.
  • Predict what you expect to happen.
  • Name the smallest material or system needed for the test.
  • Write one observable success condition.
  • Plan three fair trials.
  • Name the evidence you will capture.
  • Identify the exact next action if the result fails.

Deliver · Demonstrate · Celebrate

Show it on time and earn a Milestone Treat.

In sixty seconds, show the deliverable, explain one important design decision, identify the first November test, and name your next action. The reward recognizes preparation and responsibility, not whose design looks most polished.

Creative Robotics partners: Both students must speak and explain different parts of the shared project. Each partner also completes an individual reflection.
Reward choices: Students who meet the checkpoint may choose an individually packaged treat or a nonfood prize. Excused absences, documented accommodations, and verified technology problems receive a reasonable make up opportunity.

Your Phase 2 finish line

  • Your materials and fabrication requests are specific.
  • Your first November prototype question and three trial plan are ready.
  • You completed the lunch demonstration or an approved make up.
  • Your mentor status and required revision are recorded.
