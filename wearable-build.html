Phase 3 · Wearable Technology · Individual

Turn your denim-shirt plan into a safe, glowing build.

Complete the major artwork and sewn-light circuit on your denim shirt. Work in an order that protects wet paint, keeps circuit paths separate, and leaves the battery accessible.

✦

Your Phase 3 finish line

Major shirt artwork + a safe 4–8-light circuit.

Your circuit does not need to be perfect or invisible. It must be correctly polarized, inspectable from both sides, comfortable, steady when tested, and stored safely.

Save the hat for later. Do not cut the WS2812B programmable LED strip, fabricate its harness, or permanently install the bucket-hat electronics today. Mentors will prepare and test those removable systems before the hat integration phase.

  1. Start with the largest approved shapes

    Use the tested brush, paint amount, and color combination from Phase 2. Keep LED pads, holder access, seams, and circuit routes free of paint.

  2. Add details after the base is stable

    Use symbols and line work that remain readable from several feet away. More paint is not automatically stronger design.

  3. Stop before overworking the fabric

    If paint begins soaking, cracking, smearing, or making the fabric too stiff, stop and ask a mentor before adding another layer.

  4. Label every wet area

    Move the shirt flat to the assigned drying space with your name, pathway, wet areas, and first sewing step clearly marked.

Major Build B · Final Sewn Circuit

Connect every LED in parallel between two separate thread paths.

NEGATIVE PATH

Each LED’s + pad reaches the positive path. Each − pad reaches the negative path. The two paths never touch—on either side of the shirt.

  1. Confirm dry fabric and final placement

    Do not sew through damp paint. Recheck the 4–8 LED locations, every printed polarity mark, holder access, comfort, and the approved circuit routes.

  2. Secure components with ordinary thread first

    Use the mentor-approved nonconductive attachment method where directed so parts stay positioned while you form the electrical connections.

  3. Sew one electrical path at a time

    Complete the positive route with small, flat stitches and secure pad loops. Then use a fresh length of conductive thread for the separate negative route.

  4. Inspect the front and inside repeatedly

    After each component, check for crossings, fuzzy fibers, loose tails, weak pad contact, long snagging stitches, and pressure points against skin.

  5. Keep the holder removable and reachable

    The switch and battery opening must remain accessible. Do not seal the holder under paint, permanent fabric, or a knot that prevents repair.

Steady lightAll installed LEDs stay lit without pressing on stitches or holding the fabric still.
Safe movementGentle fabric movement does not create flicker, contact between paths, warmth, or snagging.
Accessible powerThe switch works, the battery can be removed, and the holder does not press uncomfortably.
If anything flickers, warms, smells unusual, or changes when the fabric bends: switch off immediately, remove the battery with mentor direction, mark the problem location, and repair only one connection at a time.
