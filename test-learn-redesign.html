Test. Learn. Redesign.

Use a complete-system baseline, one evidence-based redesign, and a fair retest to prove what changed—and create a ranked February punch list.

A strong redesign can simplify. Removing an unreliable feature, reducing movement, lowering brightness, clarifying a visitor cue, or making a component easier to repair may be better engineering than adding something new.

Required complete-system record

Connect every claim to something another person can see.

StageRequired statementRequired evidenceQuality check
Success condition“The complete project will _____ when _____, while remaining _____.”Written before the baseline test.Includes function plus safety, comfort/stability, clarity, or reset.
Baseline“I/we expected _____; instead I/we observed _____.”One continuous video or a clear photo sequence from setup through safe shutdown/reset.Shows the real result, including partial or failed behavior.
Learning“This evidence suggests _____ because _____.”Timestamp, close-up, trial count, user comment, reading, or other observable detail.Explains evidence without pretending to prove more than the test showed.
Redesign“I/we changed _____, not _____, because _____.”Photo, labeled sketch, or readable code screenshot.Names one high-impact variable and protects required features.
Retest“After the change, _____ became better/worse/unchanged.”Matching video/photo sequence under the original condition.Uses the same success condition and makes a fair comparison.

Write the engineering story

Use six short evidence sentences.

1. Complete-system success meant: _____.

2. During the baseline test: I/we observed _____.

3. The most important evidence was: _____.

4. That evidence suggests: _____ because _____.

5. I/we redesigned: _____.

6. The fair retest shows: _____, so I/we will _____.

Avoid unsupported claims: “It works now,” “people liked it,” or “we made it better” must be followed by a result, trial count, observation, or specific user response.

February punch list

Rank remaining work by consequence.

1

Critical

Required for safety, basic function, comfort/stability, reset, code, or a complete demonstration.

2

Important

Improves clarity, appearance, documentation, repairability, setup, pack-down, or presentation.

3

Optional

An extra feature or decoration attempted only after the complete project passes inspection and reliability testing.

Canvas evidence

Checkpoint 3: Test, Learn, Redesign

  • The complete-system success condition was written before testing.
  • The baseline shows the whole user/visitor experience through safe shutdown or reset.
  • The submission reports the real result, including failures or inconsistency.
  • The learning claim names visible evidence.
  • One meaningful redesign is named and justified.
  • The retest repeats the original condition for a fair comparison.
  • The conclusion states better, worse, unchanged, or still uncertain.
  • Wearable evidence is individual; robotics includes both names and individual partner reflections.
  • Critical, important, and optional February actions are ranked.
  • A mentor records demonstration-ready, named repair, or critical blocker.
