Wearable Exploration 1 · 25 minutes

Send a stamp from the future.

Turn one future need into a tiny stitched symbol. This is design exploration for the shared story on your shirt and hat—not a third final wearable.

✦

Goal

Stitch one small, intentional symbol

By the end, you will have a complete or honestly in-progress wooden stamp, a clear photo, and a caption explaining how the symbol connects to a future need.

What you need

▧1 inspected wooden stamp blank
≋1–3 colors of ordinary embroidery floss
↕Mentor-tested needle + threader
□Planning card + pencil
⌂Counted needle tray or pincushion
▱Named snack-size storage bag
iPiPad for evidence
Materials planned for this station

The order includes five 8-packs of wooden blanks (40 total), two organized BYMORE embroidery-floss kits, and one 100-count box of snack bags. Mentors will inspect the delivered blanks and pretest the exact needle-and-floss combination before student use.

See the program supply guide →
Non-powered station: Use only ordinary embroidery floss on the wooden blank. Conductive thread, LEDs, batteries, and powered circuits stay at the Circuit Lab station.

Learn

A tiny symbol can carry a big idea.

Start with a need

Choose one need in the future you imagine: belonging, cleaner air, safer travel, healthy oceans, access, joy, or another idea that matters to you.

Simplify the symbol

Use the hole grid to plan a small mark with about 6–12 crosses or short stitches. One clear shape is stronger than a crowded scene.

Connect the story

Your stamp may test a symbol or color for the shirt and hat, but it does not replace your required three-idea brainstorm.

Try it

Plan, stitch, and save your work

  1. Label your bag first.

    Write your name before receiving the blank, planning card, or floss.

  2. Inspect the blank.

    Check for cracks, splinters, rough edges, or blocked holes. Do not use a damaged blank; give it to a mentor.

  3. Choose a future need and symbol.

    Sketch a tiny 1–3-color path through the hole grid. Keep the plan simple enough to begin safely in this rotation.

  4. Receive the prepared tools.

    Use only the mentor-released needle and a short, pre-cut length of ordinary floss. Do not force a needle through a hole.

  5. Stitch with control.

    Pull gently, keep the front intentional, and check the back so long loose loops do not form. Ask for help if the floss tangles or the wood catches.

  6. Inspect and trim.

    Show a mentor the front and back. Secure or trim the tail as directed; craftsmanship is not graded and the stamp may remain in progress.

  7. Capture evidence.

    Take a clear front photo. Add a back photo if it helps show your choices.

  8. Bag the project; return the needle.

    Put the blank, planning card, and loose floss in your named bag. Return the needle separately to the counted tray or pincushion—never put a needle in the bag.

Needle safety: A needle is either in your hand or in the counted tray/pincushion. Stop immediately for a splinter, cracked blank, blocked hole, lost needle, or needle that must be forced. Mentors count needles at every transition.

Test

Your station is complete when…

  • Your symbol connects to a named future need.
  • You began a small, controlled motif using ordinary floss.
  • Your evidence honestly shows complete or meaningful in-progress work.
  • Your blank, card, and floss are in your named bag.
  • Your needle was returned separately and counted.

Troubleshoot

Pause instead of forcing the work.

The needle will not pass

Stop. Do not push harder. Ask a mentor to check the hole, needle eye, and floss thickness.

The floss tangles

Let the needle hang so the floss untwists, then continue with a shorter working length.

The symbol is too large

Circle the most important part of your sketch and stitch only that shape.

Time is ending

Capture honest in-progress evidence, return the needle, and store the project in your named bag.

  • Add a back photo if it shows an important stitching choice.
