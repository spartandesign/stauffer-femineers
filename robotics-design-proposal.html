Creative Robotics · One Team Proposal

Turn your idea into a plan your team can build.

Work with exactly one partner. You will plan one interactive invention, prove that it fits one Hummingbird Premium Kit, and prepare both partners to explain the complete design.

2

Your goal

Create an approval-ready team proposal.

Your proposal must show who the invention helps, how a person interacts with it, which kit components it uses, how the physical model works, and how both partners will share the work.

Start with one planned kit

Plan for one assigned Hummingbird Premium Kit. Mentors will confirm the available parts after the kits arrive and are inspected.

Choose at least one input

Light sensor, sound sensor, distance sensor, or dial sensor. The input must give the program information or start the interaction.

Plan at least two outputs

Single-color LEDs, tri-color LEDs, position servos, rotation servos with wheels, or an approved micro:bit display or sound response.

Premium Kit limits to remember

Each kit contains three single-color LEDs, two tri-color LEDs, two position servos, two rotation servos, and two wheels. The program has 10 Premium Kits, so one kit supports one team of two at a time.

Check our program supplies →
Power off before changing hardware. Connect or move sensors, LEDs, and servos only while the Hummingbird is off. Use the numbered kit assigned to your team, and ask a mentor before borrowing or substituting a component.

Proposal Part 1

Define the team and the future need

1

Team header

Write both students' names, your assigned kit number and three-word device name, and a working project title.

2

Future user or community

Name a specific person, audience, place, or community. Avoid vague answers such as "everyone."

3

Need and purpose

Complete: "Our future user needs _____ because _____. Our invention will help by _____."

Make the purpose testable: A visitor should be able to interact with the invention and notice whether it communicates, assists, teaches, welcomes, warns, organizes, or creates the experience you intended.

Proposal Part 2

Generate three ideas, then select one

Sketch three quick possibilities before choosing. Each idea should name a user, an input, two outputs, and a different physical form.

A

Idea 1

"When _____ happens, the _____ sensor notices it. Then _____ and _____ respond." Add a small thumbnail sketch.

B

Idea 2

Change the form, interaction, or user. Do not redraw the same invention with different colors.

C

Idea 3

Try a surprising alternative. Make sure it still fits one kit and can be demonstrated safely.

Choose with evidence

  • Circle the idea your team selected.
  • Explain why it best serves the future user or community.
  • Confirm that the needed inputs and outputs are available in one kit.
  • Explain why the structure and interaction are possible in the available work time.
  • If you used an idea starter, list at least three meaningful changes your team made.

Proposal Part 3

Map the complete interaction

Your system statement tells the story of the code before you open BirdBlox.

INPUT→DECISION→OUTPUT 1+OUTPUT 2→RESET
IF _____, THEN _____ AND _____; OTHERWISE _____.

Input

Name the sensor and the condition it notices. Include a starting threshold or range if your idea uses one.

Decision

State the rule the BirdBlox program will check. Describe what happens when the rule is true and when it is false.

Outputs and reset

Describe two visible, moving, or audible responses. Then explain how the system returns to a safe ready state for the next visitor.

Example

If the distance sensor detects a visitor closer than the tested distance, then a position servo opens the welcome flower and the tri-color LED turns green; otherwise the flower closes and the light returns to blue.

Proposal Part 4

Draw a labeled build plan

Your sketch must show

The full physical form, sensor location and direction, both output locations, Hummingbird controller, battery access, wire paths, moving zones, attachment points, and at least two views when one view hides important parts.

Your materials list must show

Exact kit components and quantities, structure materials, attachment materials, decoration materials, and any item that must remain removable for repair or battery access.

  • Draw the structure first.

    Use a front, side, or top view that makes the visitor interaction easy to understand.

  • Add every electronic component.

    Write the exact sensor, LED, or servo name next to its location. Label the controller and battery pack too.

  • Draw motion and wire routes.

    Use arrows for movement. Keep wires away from wheels, servo arms, pinch points, and visitor hands.

  • Show access and stability.

    Mark the opening used to reach electronics and explain how the structure will stand, roll, hang, or stay supported.

Design for repair: Do not permanently seal the controller, battery pack, connectors, sensors, or servos inside the structure.

Proposal Part 5

Storyboard the visitor experience

Draw three to five frames. Use arrows, short captions, and the visitor's point of view.

1

Ready

Show the invention waiting in its safe starting state.

2

Interact

Show what the visitor does or what changes in the environment.

3

Sense

Show the input detecting the action, distance, sound, light, or dial setting.

4

Respond

Show both planned outputs and what they communicate to the visitor.

5

Reset

Show how the project safely becomes ready for another demonstration.

Proposal Part 6

Plan the BirdBlox start and reset routine

Mentors prepare each robotics micro:bit with BirdBrain firmware. Teams program wirelessly from their iPads; do not load MakeCode onto a robotics board.

  1. Inspect while power is off.

    Check that the numbered Hummingbird, micro:bit, battery pack, sensors, and outputs match your assigned kit. Make hardware changes only while power is off.

  2. Power the Hummingbird and open BirdBlox.

    On the team iPad, choose Connect and select the assigned three-word device name. A green connection dot confirms the correct kit is connected.

  3. Run a safe startup state.

    Start with LEDs off, rotation servos stopped, and position servos at the planned safe angle before beginning the interactive program.

  4. Test one change at a time.

    Test the input first, then each output, then the full input → decision → outputs interaction. Record what happened.

  5. Reset before cleanup.

    Return moving parts and lights to their safe state, stop the program, disconnect BirdBlox, switch off the kit, and return every component to its labeled tray.

If the assigned three-word name does not appear, the green dot will not stay on, or the board behaves unexpectedly: Stop and ask a mentor. Do not change firmware or use a neighboring team's controller.

Proposal Part 7

Rotate the work

Driver

Operates the iPad or handles the current build step while explaining each action aloud. The driver does not make every decision.

Navigator

Reads the plan, checks ports and safety, predicts the result, records evidence, and asks the next testing question.

Write your rotation agreement

  • We will switch Driver and Navigator after each completed test or at least every 15–20 minutes.
  • Both partners will design, build, connect components, program, test, document, and present.
  • Both partners will be able to name the input, explain the decision, and demonstrate both outputs.
  • If we disagree, we will return to the user's need and test the smallest safe version of each idea.

Canvas Checkpoint 1

Submit these exact items

One team submission

One partner uploads the shared proposal once. Put both partners' names on every page or image and confirm that both students can open the submission.

Two individual reflections

Each partner separately submits her own short reflection. Do not copy one shared response.

Shared technical proposal checklist

  • Both names, team title, kit number, and three-word device name
  • Future user or community, need, and purpose statement
  • Three distinct ideas, selected concept, and selection explanation
  • If applicable, the three meaningful changes made to an idea starter
  • Labeled physical sketch with controller, power, input, two outputs, wires, motion, and access points
  • Input → decision → two outputs → reset statement
  • Three-to-five-frame interaction storyboard
  • Materials list with component names and quantities
  • BirdBlox connect/start/reset procedure and role-rotation agreement
  • Technology exploration evidence with clear photos or screenshots and short captions
  • Mentor decision plus any revision notes

Each partner's individual reflection

Answer all three questions: What technology did you operate or explain? What idea did you contribute? What do you need to learn next?

Ready for mentor approval?

  • Our plan has one clear future user, need, and purpose.
  • Our interaction uses at least one confirmed input and two confirmed outputs from one kit.
  • Our sketch shows safe motion, stable construction, battery access, and repair access.
  • Our storyboard matches our input → decision → outputs statement.
  • Our BirdBlox connect and reset procedure is written down.
  • Both partners can explain the entire design without reading one person's script.
  • Our exploration evidence and all proposal pages are clear and readable.

Bring the full proposal and both partners to the mentor conversation. Be ready to point to each component, demonstrate your safe starting state, and explain what you will prototype first.
