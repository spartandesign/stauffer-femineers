Explore

Run the prepared system

  1. Start with both holders off.

    Place the system on the table. Do not wear it during this exploration.

  2. Match the kit labels.

    Confirm that the micro:bit, strip, two holders, and harness belong to the same wearable station.

  3. Trace the system without changing it.

    Point to P0, the data resistor, DIN, pixel positive, shared ground, and the two separate battery holders.

  4. Inspect the connector and strip.

    Check that the JST plug is fully seated, arrows point away from DIN, and no bare wire, torn coating, or crushed pixel is visible. Ask a mentor if anything looks wrong.

  5. Power the pixels first.

    Switch on the 3×AAA pixel holder. Then connect or switch on the micro:bit 2×AAA holder. Never make or change wiring connections while either side is powered.

  6. Test all three events.

    Press A, B, and A+B. Observe color, sequence, direction, and whether every pixel responds.

  7. Change one design choice.

    On a classroom Windows laptop, adjust one color, pause, or pattern in the mentor-provided MakeCode project. Keep brightness between 30 and 60.

  8. Transfer and retest.

    With the battery holders off and disconnected as directed by your mentor, use USB to update the wearable micro:bit. Remove USB before reconnecting the battery system.

  9. Shut down safely.

    Press A+B so the pixels turn off. Switch off or disconnect the micro:bit power, then switch off the pixel power.

Test

Your exploration passes when…

  • You point to DIN and explain why P0 connects there.
  • You identify which holder powers the micro:bit and which powers the pixels.
  • You show the shared ground and explain that the positive supplies stay separate.
  • Button A runs one effect, B runs a second, and A+B turns the strip off.
  • The updated effect stays within the 30–60 brightness range.
  • You can name a condition that means stop and ask a mentor.

Troubleshoot

Observe first; do not rebuild the harness

POWER OFF→OBSERVE→CHECK ONE ITEM→ASK / RETEST

Strip stays dark

Check the pixel holder switch, battery direction, connector seating, and whether the data wire enters DIN.

micro:bit works; pixels do not

The pixel system has its own power. Check the 3×AAA holder and switch without changing any wires.

Random color or flicker

Power off both sides. Ask a mentor to check the shared ground, connector, battery level, and prepared harness.

Only some pixels work

Check the number of pixels in the program. If the count is correct, mark the first failed location and ask a mentor.

Effect moves backward

Compare the program sequence with the arrows on the strip. Confirm which physical pixel is number 0.

Warmth, odor, or damage

Stop immediately. Switch both holders off, do not touch a warm component, and call a mentor.

Mentor technical check before student use

Build and reliability-test one complete sample with the exact batteries, 10–15-pixel length, 330Ω data resistor, capacitor, and shared ground. Because the micro:bit data signal is lower-voltage than the three-cell pixel supply, revise the instructor-built interface with an appropriate logic-level shifter if the sample flickers or behaves unreliably. This is not a student wiring task.
