## 1. Phase 1 at a Glance

### Shared goals

By the end of the day, all students will be able to:

- explain the **Limitless** challenge in their own words;
- name the major stages of the engineering design process;
- identify a future person, community, need, or opportunity connected to their project;
- safely explore the core technology for their pathway;
- create a labeled, buildable project plan;
- capture useful evidence as they work;
- receive mentor approval or leave with a specific revision plan.

### Definition of success

| Pathway | Working arrangement | Phase 1 finish line |
|---|---|---|
| Wearable Technology | Individual | Each student submits one combined proposal for her own denim shirt and her own programmable bucket hat, including visuals, LED/NeoPixel plans, effects, and electronics placement. |
| Creative Robotics | Teams of exactly two | Each pair submits one proposal for an interactive future invention with a user/need, at least one input, at least two outputs, a physical concept, and an input → decision → output storyboard. |

## 2. Student Journey Through the Website

The website should reveal the minimum useful choice at each moment. Students should never need to search through the future phases to find today's task.

1. **Home** — Choose a pathway or open the design process.
2. **Start Here: Imagine the Future** — Meet the theme, central question, goals, and schedule for today.
3. **The Limitless Challenge** — Select a future area, identify a person or community, and describe a need or opportunity.
4. **Engineering Design Process** — Learn the full cycle, with emphasis on Imagine, Research, Brainstorm, and Design.
5. **Choose the correct pathway hub.**
   - Wearable students work individually.
   - Robotics students confirm a team of exactly two.
6. **Complete short technology explorations.** Each exploration follows Goal → What You Need → Learn → Try → Test → Troubleshoot → Capture for Canvas.
7. **Open the pathway proposal guide.** Turn the future idea and technology discoveries into a labeled, realistic design.
8. **Use the self-check.** Fix missing requirements before asking for mentor review.
9. **Request mentor approval.** A mentor marks Approved, Revise and Return, or Needs Technical Review.
10. **Open Canvas Checkpoint 1.** Upload the proposal evidence, verify image quality, and submit.
11. **Finish screen.** Record the first prototype task for October 5; do not begin major construction before approval.

### Persistent Phase 1 navigation

Every Phase 1 tutorial page should include:

- **Today’s Path** progress indicator;
- **Back to My Pathway** button;
- **Troubleshooting** button;
- **Capture This for Canvas** box;
- **I’m Ready for a Mentor Check** button or clear instruction;
- large touch targets and short sections suitable for iPad use.

## 4. Required Instruction and Tutorials

### Standard tutorial pattern

Every technical tutorial uses the same headings:

1. **Goal** — One sentence describing the result.
2. **What You Need** — Short visual checklist.
3. **Learn** — No more than the key ideas required for the task.
4. **Try It** — Numbered, touch-friendly steps.
5. **Test** — A visible success condition.
6. **Troubleshoot** — The three to five most likely problems.
7. **Capture This for Canvas** — Exact photo, screenshot, sketch, or note to save.

### Required Phase 1 tutorial set

| Audience | Tutorial | Student success check | Canvas capture |
|---|---|---|---|
| All | Future need → design opportunity | Names a user/community and a specific need | Future vision/problem statement |
| All | Three-idea brainstorm | Produces three meaningfully different ideas | Photo or screenshot of brainstorm |
| All | Labeled design sketch | Shows parts, placement, purpose, and notes | Clear proposal sketch |
| All | Capturing useful evidence | Photo is focused and note explains learning | One practice evidence item |
| Wearables | LED polarity and a simple circuit | Prepared LED lights and student identifies +/− | Exploration photo + one observation |
| Wearables | Conductive paths and short circuits | Student identifies two separate paths and diagnoses a sample short | Practice photo or annotated diagram |
| Wearables | micro:bit first program | Student downloads/runs a simple button or display program | MakeCode screenshot or photo |
| Wearables | NeoPixel first effect | Prepared pixels show an effect at safe brightness | Effect screenshot/photo + chosen color |
| Robotics | Hummingbird connect and reset | Team connects, runs, stops, and restarts safely | Photo + reset/start note |
| Robotics | Output explorations | Team successfully operates at least two output types | Photos or program screenshots |
| Robotics | Sensor exploration | Team explains what changes the sensor reading | Reading notes/table |
| Robotics | Input → decision → output | Team makes one sensor-triggered response work | Plain-language rule + program evidence |

## 5. Required Mentor Demonstrations

Demonstrations should be brief, visible, and rehearsed. Each should use a finished sample and one intentionally broken or incorrect sample where useful.

### Whole-group demonstrations

| Demonstration | Maximum time | Key message |
|---|---:|---|

| Engineering design in action | 7 minutes | A first idea is a starting point; testing and redesign are expected. |
| Evidence worth saving | 5 minutes | Capture clear proof while working instead of recreating it later. |
| Safety and help system | 5 minutes | Stop power before changing electronics; use the troubleshooting model; then request help. |

### Wearable demonstrations

| Demonstration | Maximum time | Required sample |
|---|---:|---|
| Sewable LED polarity | 7 minutes | Large visual polarity model and working prepared circuit |
| Conductive thread paths and shorts | 8 minutes | Practice swatch/card showing correct paths, crossing paths, loose tails, and knots |
| micro:bit and MakeCode download | 8 minutes | Projected device workflow and backup screenshots |
| NeoPixel data direction and safe brightness | 8 minutes | Prepared harness, arrows for DIN/data flow, shared-ground visual, separate-power visual |
| Wearable proposal quality | 5 minutes | One approval-ready sample and one incomplete sample |

### Robotics demonstrations

| Demonstration | Maximum time | Required sample |
|---|---:|---|
| Hummingbird connection and port map | 7 minutes | Labeled controller and safe reset/start example |
| Output sampler | 8 minutes | LED, tri-color LED, servo, and motor examples |
| Sensor values change | 7 minutes | At least two sensors shown by purpose, not only component name |
| Input → decision → output | 8 minutes | One complete interaction narrated in plain language and then shown in code |

### Demonstration delivery rules

- Use a document camera or large close-up for small electronics.
- Keep a printed or on-screen diagram visible after the live demonstration.

- Never let the demonstration become the first test of the sample.
- Prepare a non-live backup: labeled photos, short clip, or screenshot sequence.

## 10. Approval and Milestone System

### Four Phase 1 milestones

| Milestone | Evidence | Who checks it |
|---|---|---|
| M1 — Future Focus | User/community plus need/opportunity and three ideas | Student/peer self-check |
| M2 — Technology Explorer | Required pathway explorations completed and recorded | Station mentor |
| M3 — Proposal Ready | All pathway requirements shown in a labeled proposal | Student self-check, then pathway mentor |
| M4 — Build Approved | Proposal is safe, feasible, understandable, and appropriately scoped | Approving mentor; technical reviewer if flagged |

### Approval statuses

- **Approved:** Safe and feasible with available or confirmed materials. Student/team may prepare for the October 5 prototype day.
- **Approved with Notes:** Core design is feasible; small named details must be resolved before or at the start of October 5.
- **Revise and Return:** One or more required design elements are missing, unclear, too complex, or unreliable. Mentor records exact revision requests.
- **Needs Technical Review:** Power, wiring, mechanism, inventory, device compatibility, or safety requires a designated technical mentor.

### Approval criteria

A proposal is approved only when it is:

- **Purposeful:** Connected to a future vision, user, or need.
- **Complete:** Contains every pathway requirement.
- **Safe:** Uses the planned electronics and materials appropriately.
- **Feasible:** Can reasonably be completed in the remaining workdays.
- **Available:** Does not depend on unconfirmed scarce equipment.
- **Repairable:** Electronics and likely failure points remain accessible.
- **Explainable:** The student or both partners can explain how it should work.
- **Documented:** Evidence is clear enough for Canvas.

### Queue and tracking method

Use one shared tracker with one row per wearable student and one row per robotics team. Track M1–M4, approval status, missing item, assigned mentor, and next action. Students enter the mentor queue only after completing the self-check. Color alone must not carry meaning; include text labels or symbols.

### Revision rule

Mentors should give no more than three specific required revisions at one time. Students revise, highlight what changed, and return to the same mentor when possible. No major construction begins under a Revise and Return status.

## 13. Risks and Practical Controls

| Risk | Control |
|---|---|
| Too many students waiting for equipment | Base rotations on verified simultaneous capacity; use paired observation roles that switch; keep proposal work available between rotations. |
| Mentor approval bottleneck | Require self-check first; use a visible queue; allow pathway mentors to approve ordinary proposals and escalate only technical exceptions. |
| Students design beyond available hardware | Give robotics teams an inventory-based component menu; mark unconfirmed parts clearly; approve only confirmed configurations. |
| Website reading replaces building time | Keep pages short, use diagrams/checklists, and pair each tutorial with immediate action. |
| Weak Canvas evidence | Put exact capture instructions inside each tutorial and schedule evidence review before cleanup. |
| One robotics partner dominates | Require role rotation and have each partner explain the entire interaction during approval. |
| Hat power system is misunderstood | Use only prepared harnesses; show shared ground and separate positive supplies visually; defer full integration instruction to the January 12 integration phase. |
| Network or login failure | Keep printable/offline directions, downloaded starter programs, paper proposal forms, and a later upload path. |
| Technology exploration consumes proposal time | Use strict rotation timing and prepared starter programs; stop optional extensions when the core success check is met. |
