Major Build A · Physical Structure

Construct from the load-bearing form outward.

1Stable baseSupports the project and resists tipping.
2Moving mechanismTurns freely by hand without binding.
3Accessible mountsHold parts without sealing them inside.
4Wire routesAvoid motion, pinch points, and visitors.
5Purposeful finishCommunicates the idea after function is safe.
  1. Compare the proposal with the Phase 2 prototype

    Mark the tested dimensions, mechanism position, sensor direction, visitor action, and the change that testing showed was necessary.

  2. Build a stable base and main form

    Use only released structure and attachment materials. Check balance before adding components or tall decorative parts.

  3. Construct and hand-test each mechanism

    Move hinges, wheels, levers, doors, flowers, pointers, or other mechanisms by hand. Nothing should scrape, bind, strike the structure, or require force.

  4. Mark service zones

    Outline where the controller, battery, ports, sensor, outputs, and moving parts must remain reachable. Do not cover these zones with permanent decoration.

Stop condition: pause if the structure tips, the motion pinches or strikes, a mechanism needs force, the sensor is blocked, or a planned mount would trap a component.

Major Build B · Component Mounting

Temporarily mount, test, then strengthen only what works.

◉SensorFaces the visitor or condition it must detect.
▣Controller + powerReachable for ports, switch, reset, and battery changes.
↻Servo or motorAligned with the mechanism and free of side pressure.
✦Light or second outputVisible, meaningful, and protected from strain.
〰Wire routesSecured away from motion, sharp bends, and visitor hands.
  1. Power off the entire Hummingbird system

    Hardware connections and changes happen only while the kit is off. Match every component and numbered port to the approved plan.

  2. Use adjustable mounts first

    Use the mentor-approved removable or adjustable attachment method until the position, angle, motion, and wire slack pass testing.

  3. Check moving clearance

    Rotate or move the mechanism slowly by hand through its full range. Wires, fasteners, decorations, and fingers stay outside the moving zone.

  4. Create a service path

    A mentor must be able to remove or reconnect the controller, power, input, and outputs without tearing apart the invention.

  1. Open BirdBlox on the team iPad

    Connect only to your assigned three-word device name and confirm the green connection indicator. Never load MakeCode onto the robotics micro:bit.

  2. Run the safe startup state

    Begin with rotation servos stopped, position servos at the approved safe angle, and lights or sound in the planned ready state.

  3. Test input, Output 1, and Output 2 separately

    Confirm the physical installation did not change the sensor reading, block a light, overload a mechanism, or reverse intended motion.

  4. Run the complete interaction three times

    Use the same visitor action and observe response clarity, movement, stability, timing, wire clearance, and reset.

  5. Change one variable when needed

    Adjust one threshold, timing value, mount position, mechanism dimension, or program block. Retest before making another change.

Stop and power off if a servo stalls, a wheel cannot turn, the project tips, a wire is pinched, a component becomes warm, or motion could touch a person.
