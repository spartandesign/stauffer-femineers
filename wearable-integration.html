Layer 1 · Finish the denim shirt

Complete the shortest safe path to a reliable shirt.

  1. Use the December repair note.

    Repair the named stitch, pad loop, circuit separation, protective layer, artwork area, or holder access before doing optional decoration.

  2. Inspect both sides before power.

    Check positive and negative paths, tails, knots, polarity, fuzzy fibers, crossings, pressure points, and hidden hardware.

  3. Run the steady-light test.

    After mentor approval, every installed LED should remain steadily lit for the mentor-set interval without warmth, odor, flicker, or shifting.

  4. Run a gentle movement and comfort test.

    Use the approved movement while another student observes snagging, flicker, rubbing, sharpness, blocked access, or artwork cracking.

  5. Record the shirt status.

    Mark pass, one named repair, or mentor hold. Remove or isolate the CR2032 exactly as directed before beginning hat integration.

Layer 2 · Build the programmable bucket hat

Install the removable system in a safe order.

REMOVABLE CHANNEL10–12 pixels · arrows point away from DINflat route · no tight folds · light faces outward
micro:bit + 2×AAAMakeCode controller · reachable switch
3×AAA pixel powerseparate positive supply · reachable switch
SERVICE PATH STAYS OPENJST-SM · mentor harness · P0 data · shared ground · removable parts
  1. Confirm the released segment.

    Match your label, planned pixel count, DIN end, arrow direction, tested connector, and mentor initials. Do not substitute another student’s segment or harness.

  2. Fit the strip into the removable route.

    Keep the strip flat and unstrained. Do not pierce the strip, fold sharply across a pixel, block light, or permanently glue electronics into the hat.

  3. Install accessible controller and power pockets.

    Use the approved balanced locations. Switches, connectors, and battery openings must remain reachable without placing hard parts against the head.

  4. Connect only after mentor inspection.

    With both supplies off, the mentor checks the harness, P0 data path, DIN direction, shared ground, and separate positive supplies before authorizing connection.

  5. Run a stationary power test.

    Place the hat on the stand, not a person. Start with low brightness and the approved simple program. Stop for heat, odor, unexpected color, flicker, reset, loose connection, or damaged parts.

Choose one meaningful redesign

Use the weakest test result—not the easiest decoration.

Comfort

Move or cushion a pocket, rebalance the two sides, flatten an attachment, or protect a hard part without trapping electronics.

Reliability

Support a removable connection, correct strip strain, repair one shirt path, simplify a light sequence, or improve switch access.

Clarity

Revise color choices, pattern timing, artwork contrast, or the explanation so another person understands the future theme.

Repairability

Open a blocked service path, improve labels, make a component easier to remove, or separate stored power safely.
