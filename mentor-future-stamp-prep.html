Rehearse the exact materials.

  1. Inspect first. Sort blanks into READY and INSPECT. Do not hand out rough or damaged wood or ask students to force a blocked hole.
  2. Test the whole stitch. Thread the actual needle, pass the threaded eye through several holes, and make a short motif. Adjust floss thickness or use a suitable tested needle; preserve a comfortable fit.
  3. Make two samples. Finish a tiny motif and prepare the same motif partly stitched. Practice saying, “A meaningful beginning counts.”
  4. Set the tray. Count needles before students arrive; put snips within mentor control. Label bags before issuing a needle. Rehearse collecting and recounting everything in the last three minutes.

Show one stitch, then let students try.

  1. Thread and start. Neatly trimmed floss is easier to thread; use the tested threader if needed. Leave a short working tail through the eye. On the back, hold the starting tail flat and capture it beneath the first few back stitches; a mentor can help with the first stitch.
  2. Make the first diagonal. Bring the needle from back to front through hole 1 (bottom left), then front to back through hole 2 (top right). Pull gently, leaving the starting tail secured on the back.
  3. Complete the cross. Come up through hole 3 (top left) and down through hole 4 (bottom right). Keep the same top-diagonal direction as the motif grows.
  4. Finish or pause. On the back, weave the tail under several existing stitches and have the mentor trim it. If there are not enough stitches to secure it, let the mentor secure the loose tail for storage. Return the needle separately.

Watch the technique; adapt to the wood.

Keep the task small and the needle controlled.

If you see…Do this
Needle or threaded eye will not passStop. Swap to the pretested combination or paper-grid task; never force it.
TanglingShorten the working length and let the thread untwist while the needle stays controlled.
Too much design for the timeCircle the strongest feature; stitch that. Accept an intentional beginning.
Lost needle, crack, or splinterPause the station. Account for the needle or isolate the blank before resuming.
