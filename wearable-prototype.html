Prototype A · Paint and Material Test

Test color, coverage, and flexibility on scrap fabric.

  1. Copy two small details

    Choose two colors, symbols, line styles, or layered effects from your approved design. Keep each test small.

  2. Label the test

    Write your name and note the brush, paint amount, and color combination beside the swatch or on a paper label.

  3. Let it dry flat

    Do not touch, fold, or stack wet paint. Place the swatch in the assigned drying area.

  4. Inspect after drying

    Check coverage, stiffness, cracking, color contrast, and whether the detail still looks clear from several feet away.

  5. Choose one change

    Adjust paint amount, brush size, spacing, color, layering, or symbol size before painting the final shirt.

Prototype B · One-Light Circuit Swatch

Prove that your stitching can carry power safely.

POSITIVE PATHHolder +→LED +
NEGATIVE PATHHolder −→LED −
  1. Draw first

    On the felt, mark the battery holder, one LED, a positive route, and a separate negative route. The two paths must not touch or cross.

  2. Switch power off

    Keep the CR2032 holder off while threading, sewing, tying knots, changing a component, or inspecting the back.

  3. Sew secure connections

    Use tight, repeated loops around each approved pad. Keep tails short and contained.

  4. Inspect before power

    Ask a partner or mentor to trace both paths and check polarity, loose tails, crossings, and possible shorts.

  5. Test and change one thing

    Switch on only after inspection. If the LED fails or flickers, switch off, check one connection at a time, record the change, and retest.

Needle safety: Return the needle to its marked container whenever it is not in your hand. Never leave it loose in fabric, on a chair, or under paper.

Prototype C · First Programmable Light Effect

Make one planned effect run—and make it stop.

1ProgramMakeCode on a Windows laptop
→

→
3ConnectMentor-prepared harness, power off
→
4TestLow brightness, then record
  1. Inspect the prepared system

    Confirm the assigned “W” micro:bit, first-pixel direction, connectors, separate battery holders, and off switches. Do not cut, splice, or open the harness.

  2. Program Button A

    Create one signature color or short effect connected to your future vision. Use low brightness and a short duration during testing.

  3. Program lights off

    Add the approved stop behavior so A+B turns every pixel off. Test the stop before repeating the effect.

  4. Run three trials

    Check whether the effect starts correctly, looks intentional, ends safely, and repeats consistently.

  5. Record one code change

    Change timing, brightness, color, sequence, or number of pixels based on what you observed—not only personal preference.

Stop immediately if a battery holder, wire, connector, board, or pixel becomes warm; if lights flicker unpredictably; or if you smell anything unusual. Switch both power supplies off and call a mentor.
