Design it digitally. Make it safely.

Use Tinkercad on your iPad to imagine and refine a custom part. Students create the design; mentors approve the file; trained adults operate restricted machines; students test and improve the result.

Our fabrication promise

Design → approve → fabricate → test → revise

A digital file is never permission to use a machine. Every custom part must support the project, fit the approved plan, pass a file check, and enter the mentor fabrication queue.

With 50 members and three mentors: restricted machines will not operate as open workday stations. Most laser, CNC, 3D-printing, and woodshop jobs will be prepared by trained adults through the request queue.

Phase 1 · Digital Concept

STL or SVG?

Choose the file by how the part will be made

A three-dimensional object

Export STL

Use for a bracket, spacer, sensor mount, cable guide, button, decorative object, or other part printed on a Bambu P2S.

A flat outline or panel

Export SVG

Use for a sign, flat robot panel, decorative overlay, gear outline, template, or base that may be reviewed for the laser cutter or X-Carve Pro.

An SVG is a design file—not a machine job. A trained operator still chooses the approved material, thickness, bit, cutting depth, laser settings, hold-down method, and toolpath. Tinkercad does not create the final X-Carve or laser instructions.
Prototype before exporting: Make the part from cardboard first when possible. Check size, access, motion, comfort, and connection points before using machine time or final material.

Phase 2 · Fabrication-Ready File Check

Turn the concept into a measured request

STL checklist

  • Designed at full size in millimeters
  • Wall thickness and openings are intentional
  • Clearance is included around real components
  • Intended solid shapes are grouped
  • STL opens and shows the expected complete part

SVG checklist

  • Designed at full size with overall dimensions recorded
  • Flat profile uses clean, closed outlines
  • Duplicate and accidental overlapping shapes are removed
  • Material and thickness are requested—not assumed
  • SVG opens and shows the expected outline

Name and submit the file clearly

Filename pattern: TeamName_PartName_v1.stl or TeamName_PartName_v1.svg

  • Exported STL or SVG
  • Tinkercad share link
  • Screenshot with dimensions visible
  • Part purpose and project location
  • Requested material, color, thickness, and quantity
  • Photo of the cardboard test or explanation of why one is not practical

Machine access

Different tools require different supervision

ChompSaw · Student station

Students may cut approved cardboard after orientation and a safety check. Use cardboard 3 mm or thinner, keep it flat, cut away from the body, switch off between cuts, and follow the dust-drawer schedule.

Six Bambu P2S printers · Managed queue

Students design and submit STL files. Mentors approve slicing and start jobs. Students may collect a print only after the machine and build plate have cooled and a mentor releases it.

Laser cutter · Trained adult

Students submit approved SVG designs. A trained adult verifies the machine-specific material list, exhaust, air assist, interlocks, settings, fire readiness, and watches the entire job.

X-Carve Pro · Trained adult

Students submit the design and observe outside the marked zone. A trained adult handles the bit, clamps, zeroing, dust collection, toolpath, start, stop, and emergency procedures.

Bandsaw + drill press · Restricted

Students plan and mark the work. A trained adult operates unless district approval, a tool-specific student certification, functioning guards, and direct supervision are all in place.

Other woodshop tools · Tool-by-tool decision

Access is never granted by a general “woodshop” permission. Mentors select the safest suitable process and follow the manufacturer and district procedure for that exact tool.

Stop conditions: Missing guard, unknown material, failed exhaust or dust collection, unclear dimensions, loose stock, damaged tool, missing approval, crowded safety zone, or no trained operator means the job does not run.

Your request is ready when…

  • The part supports an approved project need.
  • The file and Tinkercad link use the required names.
  • Dimensions, quantity, material request, and project location are clear.
  • A cardboard test or reason for skipping it is included.
  • You understand that mentors may revise the process, material, size, or schedule.
  • You have a useful project task to do while the request is in the queue.
