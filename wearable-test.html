A safer shirt + one evidence-based repair + a hat mounting mockup.

Triage before power

Photograph and inspect the stored shirt from both sides.

◫

Artwork

Check for damp paint, cracking, peeling, stiffness, sticking, or design areas that hide LED pads and holder access.

〰

Conductive paths

Look for broken stitches, loose pad loops, fuzzy fibers, long tails, crossings, snagging, or contact between positive and negative.

✦

LEDs

Confirm every module is secure, correctly oriented, uncracked, uncovered, and not pressing sharply toward skin.

▣

Holder + comfort

Check switch access, battery opening, pocket or protective layer, pressure points, and movement around seams or bends.

Keep the battery out during inspection and repair. If paint is still damp, paths appear to touch, hardware is damaged, or anything smells unusual, move the shirt to the mentor inspect area.

Repair one variable

Trace the symptom to one likely location before sewing or replacing anything.

BATTERY OUT→MARK THE SYMPTOM→CHECK ONE LOCATION→MENTOR INSPECTS→RETEST

One LED is dark

Check its printed polarity, both pad loops, the path immediately before and after it, and whether the module is damaged.

Light changes when pressed

Mark the exact pad or stitch that responds to pressure. Repair that connection instead of tightening every stitch.

Several LEDs are dark

Trace the shared positive and negative paths from the holder toward the first failed area. Look for one broken or loose common connection.

Flicker during movement

Locate the bend, seam, long stitch, tail, or connection that moves. Add only the approved support or resew that section.

Paths may touch

Do not power. Separate, insulate, reroute, or resew only with mentor approval after inspecting both fabric sides.

Still unclear

Move the shirt to the inspect area. A known-good battery or LED is a mentor diagnostic tool, not a reason to replace parts randomly.

Needle rule: the battery stays out and the needle returns to its marked container whenever it leaves your hand. A mentor repeats the power gate before every retest.

January preparation · Programmable Bucket Hat

Mock up the removable mounting system—without using the final LED strip.

REMOVABLE CHANNEL10–12-pixel routepaper, ribbon, or mentor-provided mock strip only
micro:bit + 2×AAAreachable switch / connector
3×AAA pixel powerbalanced opposite side
OPEN SERVICE PATHDIN end · connector · wire route · no sharp bends
  1. Use a non-electronic stand-in

    Measure the approved 10–12-pixel route using paper, ribbon, or the mentor-provided mock strip. Do not cut the purchased WS2812B strip.

  2. Mock up a removable channel or attachment points

    Use ordinary sewing supplies or removable fasteners approved by a mentor. The final strip must slide out or detach without damaging the hat.

  3. Balance the controller and power locations

    Mark two reachable inside positions that do not create a pressure point, block vision, pull the hat sideways, or trap the switches.

  4. Trace the future data path

    Label the micro:bit, P0, mentor-prepared harness, DIN end, arrow direction, shared ground, and separate positive supplies on a photo or sketch.

  5. Fit-check without electronics

    Use only safe stand-ins during fit and movement checks. Record pinching, imbalance, blocked access, loose attachment, or an overly tight channel.

January boundary: mentors release tested strip segments and harnesses only after confirming the sample system, inventory, battery plan, and any needed logic-level interface.
