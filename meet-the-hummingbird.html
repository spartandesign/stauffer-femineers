What is it?

One controller connects ideas to actions

◉INPUTSThe invention senses

Light, sound, distance, a dial, buttons, or movement

→
◆CODEBirdBlox decides

Your iPad sends block-based instructions by Bluetooth

→
OUTPUTSLights change
OUTPUTSServos move or sound plays
↺RESET: Return to a safe ready state for the next visitor.
Think of the Hummingbird as a bridge: craft materials give the invention its form, sensors notice what is happening, your program makes a decision, and outputs create a response.

Getting to know the parts

Explore the Premium Kit

Controller + micro:bit

The Hummingbird Bit Controller connects the electronic parts. Its micro:bit acts as the processor and Bluetooth link.

Inputs

Use the light, sound, distance, or dial sensor—or the micro:bit’s buttons and built-in sensors—to notice an interaction.

Outputs

Use single-color LEDs, tri-color LEDs, position servos, rotation servos, the LED screen, or sound to respond.

Our Stauffer workflow

What students do—and what mentors prepare

Mentors prepare

Use a classroom Windows laptop to load BirdBrain’s Bluetooth firmware, label each robotics micro:bit and Premium Kit, and confirm the controller’s three-word device name.

Students program

Teams of two connect to their assigned kit in BirdBlox on an iPad, look for the correct device name and green connection dot, and rotate Driver and Navigator roles.

Important: Do not load a MakeCode program onto a robotics micro:bit. That replaces the BirdBrain firmware needed for BirdBlox. If the device does not connect, stop and ask a mentor.

Before choosing a project

Complete one short capability tour

  • Open the official Hummingbird overview and name one capability that surprises you.
  • Open the kit contents and identify one input and two outputs your team might use.
  • Preview one BirdBlox lesson or project example.
  • Explain your idea as: “When the visitor ___, the invention will ___ and ___.”
  • Record the link or a screenshot as exploration evidence for your proposal.
