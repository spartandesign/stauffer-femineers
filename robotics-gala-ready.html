Failure-recovery plan

Prepare one safe response for each likely problem.

BirdBlox disconnects

Keep hands out of the moving zone, stop the visitor, follow the safe power routine, and reconnect only to the assigned three-word device with mentor approval.

Sensor is unreliable

Use the approved alternate cue, threshold, or mentor-controlled demonstration. Do not move components while powered or borrow another team’s sensor.

Motion becomes unsafe

Stop the program and power off. Present lights/sound, a static mechanism, or the evidence video instead of forcing the servo or movement.

Project cannot reset

Stop public interaction. Use the documented shutdown routine and explain the design with the project card/video until a mentor approves a repair.

Robotics gallery rehearsal

Rotate both public roles and complete the full visitor cycle.

◎Presenter / Greeter

Welcomes the visitor, explains the future need and safe action, watches the visitor boundary, and shares the testing/redesign story.

↔
⌨Operator / Resetter

Connects and starts safely, monitors hardware and the green dot, runs or observes the interaction, resets, recovers, and shuts down.

  1. Invite safely.

    Use one sentence and the project sign to show where the visitor stands and what action to take.

  2. Run without coaching.

    The visitor follows the cue; the team observes instead of changing the instructions midway.

  3. Explain the engineering.

    Name the future user, input, two outputs, important test failure, redesign, and retest result in about 45 seconds.

  4. Reset for the next visitor.

    Return the project to ready, check the boundary and structure, and confirm the next interaction can begin.

  5. Switch roles and repeat.

    Both partners must greet, explain, operate, reset, answer one question, and follow the failure plan.
