Phase 2 · Prototype Evidence

Prove what you learned.

A prototype is not complete when it looks impressive. It is complete when your evidence shows what you tried, what happened, what you changed, and what you will do next.

Use this test record

Run at least three trials when time and safety allow.

TrialWhat did you do?What did you expect?What happened?What will you change?
1First complete testWrite before testingRecord the real resultName one change
2Retest after the changePredict the new resultRecord the real resultKeep or revise?
3Reliability checkShould it work again?Record consistencyPhase 3 next step
Honest results are useful results. Do not write “worked” when a light flickered, a servo jammed, a stitch loosened, or the program responded only sometimes. Specific observations lead to stronger redesigns.

First-test note

Use four sentences to explain the learning.

1. Expected: I expected our/my prototype to _____ when _____.

2. Observed: During the first test, I observed _____.

3. Changed: I changed _____ because _____.

4. Next: Before major construction, I need to _____.

Exact pathway evidence

Wearable individual evidence

  • Paint/material test photo
  • One-light circuit-swatches photo with power off
  • Photo or video showing the LED lighting
  • NeoPixel effect video and readable MakeCode screenshot
  • Individual test record and first-test note

Robotics team evidence

  • One photo of the complete physical prototype
  • Short video showing input and both outputs
  • Readable BirdBlox program screenshot
  • Shared three-trial test record and first-test note
  • One sentence from each partner naming her contribution

Save files so they are easy to identify.

Phase 2 Prototype Evidence

  • Every image and video is upright, focused, and bright enough.
  • The prototype and important parts are visible.
  • Code blocks can be read when the screenshot is opened.
  • The test record contains real observations, not only “worked” or “didn’t work.”
  • The first-test note names a specific Phase 3 next step.
  • Your name—or both robotics team members—is included.
