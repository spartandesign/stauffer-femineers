Progress is not the same as completion. It is acceptable to show unfinished work. Clear evidence makes the current condition, remaining problem, and next action understandable.

Capture before cleanup

Take these four pieces of evidence in this order.

1

Overall photo

Include the entire shirt or robotics structure. Use a clear background and show enough context to understand the scale.

2

Engineering close-up

Show the strongest detail or most important problem: paint, stitches, paths, mechanism, mount, sensor, or wire access.

3

Current test

Record a short video or photo sequence only after the project passes the appropriate power and safety check.

4

Readable code

When programming is part of today’s work, save a screenshot where the important blocks and values can be read.

Review photo and video tips

Plan-versus-build note

Explain one meaningful choice in four sentences.

1. Planned: My/our approved proposal showed _____.

2. Learned: The prototype or today’s construction revealed _____.

3. Changed: I/we changed _____ because _____.

4. Result: The current evidence shows _____.

No meaningful change? Explain one tested choice you kept and cite the evidence that supported keeping it. Do not invent a change to make the reflection sound more dramatic.

  • The test evidence is honest and was captured after the correct safety check.
  • The plan-versus-build note gives a specific reason supported by evidence.
  • The mentor status matches the recorded conference decision.
  • The December 7 first step begins with a concrete action and names the part or subsystem.
  • Your name—or both robotics team members—is included.
