Wearable Technology · Individual Proposal

Turn your future vision into a buildable plan.

Your proposal must show how one personal theme connects two required projects: a painted denim shirt with sewn LEDs and a programmable NeoPixel bucket hat. A mentor should be able to understand your idea, materials, electronics, comfort plan, and first build step without guessing.

✦

Your goal

Prepare one complete proposal for both wearables.

You are working individually. Your sketches and explanations should be your own, even when you brainstorm or test technology beside classmates.

Part 1 · Your Design Story

Begin with meaning, not decoration.

1

Future vision

Write 2–4 sentences: What future do you imagine, who is part of it, and why does it matter to you?

2

Three visual ideas

Draw or describe at least three possible symbols, patterns, scenes, or light behaviors before choosing your strongest combination. Your Future Stamp may test one of these symbols, but it does not replace the three-idea brainstorm.

3

Shared design language

Choose a color palette, repeated shape or symbol, and one sentence explaining how the shirt and hat belong together.

Using an idea starter? Name the starter, then change at least three meaningful parts—such as the purpose, symbols, layout, color meaning, or light behavior. Color changes alone do not count as three changes.

Part 2 · LED Denim Shirt

Show the artwork and the circuit.

Your shirt sketch should make the light locations look intentional. Plan approximately 4–8 sewable LEDs, then show how the two conductive-thread paths stay separate.

Front view

Draw the artwork, color areas, LED locations, and any visible symbols or words. Number each LED so your sequence is easy to explain.

Back or inside view

Draw the positive and negative thread-path ideas, switched CR2032 holder, battery-access opening, and any protective pocket or fabric layer.

A

Artwork plan

Label your soft-fabric-paint colors and show which areas must dry before electronics work begins.

B

Light plan

Place 4–8 Leyal LilyPad LEDs where light strengthens the meaning. Mark every + and − terminal.

C

Access plan

Place the switched CR2032 holder where you can reach the switch and battery without uncomfortable pressure.

Shirt safety check: The positive and negative conductive-thread paths must never touch or cross. Plan secure knots, short clean routes, and an inside layer that keeps thread tails and hardware away from skin.

Part 3 · Programmable Bucket Hat

Show the pixels, program, power, and comfort.

Plan 10–12 WS2812B NeoPixels on a removable route by default; a mentor may approve up to 15 after checking inventory. Your sketch must show the outside design and where every removable electronics part will sit.

Label this system on your hat sketch

micro:bit P0→Mentor-prepared harness→DIN arrow→10–12 pixels default
  • 2×AAA holder powers the micro:bit controller.
  • Switched 3×AAA holder separately powers the NeoPixels.
  • The mentor-prepared harness provides the approved data connection and shared ground.
  • The two positive power supplies stay separate.

Outside view

Inside view

Draw two electronics pockets on opposite sides for balance, accessible switches, JST-SM connection, and removable ribbon channel or fabric loops.

Part 5 · Wearability + Repair

Design for a real person to wear it.

1

Comfort

Keep hard parts away from pressure points. Show how pockets, backing fabric, and balanced placement protect the wearer.

2

Access

Show how a mentor can reach the switches, batteries, connectors, micro:bit, and first pixel without undoing the decoration.

3

Removal

Explain how electronics come out before cleaning, repair, or storage. Permanent glue over electronics is not an approved plan.

Evidence to capture: Make one labeled exploration-evidence page with (1) a front photo of your complete or meaningful in-progress Future Stamp, (2) your combined sewable-LED/conductive-thread circuit test, (3) your micro:bit button test, and (4) your prepared NeoPixel-strip test. Add a short caption to each: what you tried, what happened, and what you learned. The stamp caption also explains what the symbol represents and how it may inform the shirt-and-hat theme.

Part 6 · Mentor-Ready Check

Could another person build from your plan?

MeaningfulYour future vision is personal and every major design choice supports it.
CompleteBoth required projects, all sketch views, light counts, program behaviors, and materials are included.
BuildableYour plans use confirmed supplies, fit the time available, and identify a sensible first prototype task.
SafePower, polarity, separate thread paths, mentor-only harness work, and switch access are clearly shown.
WearableThe electronics are balanced, protected from skin, removable, and reachable for battery changes.
ExplainableYou can describe what every light and program behavior means without reading your whole proposal.
Practice your 60-second explanation: “My future vision is… My shirt communicates it by… My hat adds… The most important safety or comfort choice is… My first prototype task will be…”

Exact Canvas Deliverables

Submit one individual wearable proposal containing all of these items.

  • Your name, future-vision statement, and at least three visual brainstorm ideas.
  • Your chosen color palette, repeated symbol or shape, and explanation of how the two projects connect.
  • Denim-shirt front and back/inside sketches with 4–8 LED locations, every +/− mark, conductive-thread-path idea, switched CR2032 holder, battery access, and painting plan.
  • Bucket-hat outside and inside sketches with a 10–12 NeoPixel path (or mentor-approved path up to 15), DIN/data-direction arrow, Button A effect, Button B effect, A+B lights off, two balanced electronics pockets, switches, and removable mounting plan.
  • A materials list using confirmed program supplies, including installation and decorative materials.
  • If requesting a custom fabricated part: Tinkercad screenshot/link, dimensions, purpose, and planned STL or SVG output.
  • One labeled technology-exploration evidence page showing your Future Stamp, combined LED/conductive-thread circuit, micro:bit test, and NeoPixel test. Each item has a short caption explaining what you tried, what happened, and what you learned; the stamp caption also explains the symbol and its connection to your proposal.
  • Your mentor decision—Approved, Approved with Notes, Revise and Return, or Needs Technical Review—and any required changes.
  • Your first prototype question and three trial plan for the November 16 workday.

Before uploading: Make sure every image is upright and readable, your name appears on the proposal, and your original photos and sketches are saved.
